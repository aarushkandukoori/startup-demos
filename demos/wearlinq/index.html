Streaming 6-Lead QRS Detector

A simulated two-channel chest patch streams at 250 Hz. Four more limb leads are derived from it, a streaming Pan–Tompkins detector picks QRS complexes out of the noise, and every beat is scored against the ground truth the simulator knows it wrote.

Channels I and II are measured. III, aVR, aVL and aVF are computed from them (III = II−I, aVR = −(I+II)/2, aVL = I−II/2, aVF = II−I/2) — the same trick a two-electrode patch uses to report six leads.

Live scoring against ground truth

The simulator wrote every R peak, so the detector can be scored exactly. A detection counts as a hit when its estimated R location lands within ±150 ms of a true beat — the tolerance the standard beat-by-beat benchmarks use. The first 2.5 s are excluded as detector warm-up.

The AF call fires when irregularity passes 10%, measured only across intervals between two beats the classifier believes are normal — otherwise ectopy masquerades as irregularity. P-wave energy is shown but deliberately not used: switch between normal sinus and atrial fibrillation and watch it barely move, which is why a patch-sized AF check is built on intervals rather than on P waves.

The beat classifier here is a 5-feature softmax with hand-set weights (QRS width, prematurity, P-wave energy, R amplitude), not a trained network. It is in the demo to show where the classification stage sits in the stream and how noise degrades it — not to claim an accuracy number.

Changing anything here clears the running scores, so the numbers always describe the current settings.

The simulated QRS axis is about 58°, so aVL sits nearly perpendicular to it and is close to isoelectric. Pick aVL and watch sensitivity fall — a lead with no R wave has nothing to detect.

Nothing arrives inside 400 ms at rest, so this looks inert — until you push the rate past 150 bpm, where a long blanking window starts swallowing real beats.

The whole precision/recall dial. Raise it with motion artifact turned up and false positives fall away; raise it with search-back off and real beats start going missing.

Re-scans at half the threshold once 1.66 average RR passes with no beat. Set the threshold coefficient to 0.70 and toggle this: sensitivity swings by roughly 18 points.

Rejects a peak within 360 ms of the last QRS whose steepest slope is under half of that QRS's. Silent in lead II, where the T wave is nowhere near the R in slope. Switch to aVL, where it is, and it starts removing false positives.
