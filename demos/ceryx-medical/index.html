One synthetic breathing signal drives three pacing controllers at the same time — fixed rate, rate-adaptive, and a closed loop that modulates every beat with the sensed breath. Change the breathing, the demand, the coupling depth or the sense-to-pace latency and watch what each one delivers.

Breathing-locked rate is the part of the paced rate that is genuinely in phase with the breath: the slope of a least-squares fit of instantaneous rate against the breathing waveform, times that waveform's RMS. Zero means the pacing ignores breathing. Negative means the loop is running late enough that the heart is speeding up during expiration instead — push the latency slider up and watch it cross zero. Three things to try. Drag metabolic demand up and the fixed-rate row falls behind on output while the other two climb over about half a minute. Drag respiratory swing and only the coupled row's breathing-locked rate moves. Drag sense→pace latency past a quarter of a breath and the coupled row's advantage decays, then inverts — that number is the whole argument for keeping the loop fast.

What is actually being computed

  • Breathing is a zero-mean airflow-shaped wave — a short inspiratory hump over 40% of the cycle, a longer, shallower expiratory trough — with seeded breath-to-breath period jitter.
  • Sensing passes that wave through a transport delay, a slow wandering artefact, and a 70 ms one-pole filter. The controller only ever sees the filtered estimate, so its own latency is part of the loop.
  • Pacing is integrate-and-fire: each controller advances a phase at the commanded rate and emits a beat when the phase wraps, so intervals fall out of the command rather than being scheduled.
  • Haemodynamics is a filling curve — stroke volume saturates as 1 − e−RR/470 ms — times a ±14% respiratory preload swing and a contractility term that rises with demand. Output is the stroke volumes actually ejected in the window, divided by the window.
  • Nothing here is measured or clinical. The haemodynamic model is a teaching-grade approximation: it deliberately does not produce a large output jump for respiratory coupling, because that would be a claim this simulation cannot support. What it does show is the phase relationship, and how latency destroys it.
